Five NeoForge 1.21.1 mods live in `mods/`, built together with Gradle:

| Mod | Folder | What it does |
|---|---|---|
| EXP Research | `mods/expresearch` | Research Table, blueprints, advancement-style research tree (drawn from vanilla's own GUI art, so the /uitheme looks restyle it). Research costs XP points and sample items; a researched blueprint is right-clicked to unlock recipes (Chapters stages) for the whole FTB team. Hides locked items in EMI. |
| Create: Empyrean Engines | `mods/emp_engines` | Navigation Console, launch platform, Resonant Antennas and Resonance Pylons (3 tiers), the launch sequence, and timed runs into the Etheric Skies. |
| Silent Apotheosis Raids | `mods/silentraids` | Night Raids (sieges) every 5 nights, an automatic Apotheosis world tier per player, and levels (with gem sockets) for Silent Gear tools, weapons and armour. |
| Weathered Maps | `mods/weatheredmaps` | Sealed treasure maps found in peaceful places; unrolled, each marks the nearest unclaimed dungeon, pillager base or hostile fort. |
| Does Enchanting Overhaul | `mods/researchenchanting` | The vanilla enchanting table becomes a research desk: bind books into research foci (Echo), research enchantment levels with farmable resources, shelve foci in chiseled bookshelves and enchant with exactly what you pick. Replaces Apothic Enchanting and Create: Enchantment Industry. |
| Tiered Foundry | `mods/tieredfoundry` | A brick foundry that grows in tiers (vanilla bricks and solid fuel heaters, a Create fan for alloying, fire bricks and lava heaters), with drains, taps, casting tables and basins, gold and sand casts, Silent Gear part casting and coating. Replaces Productive Metalworks and Silent Gear Metalworks. |

## Building

    .\tools\build-mods.ps1

This updates and downloads the pack's mods (the mods compile against those exact jars),
builds them all, and copies the jars into `pack\mods`. If it fails, send
`mods-errors.txt` or `build-mods.log`.

## Content that lives in the pack, not the mods

- Research tree: `pack/kubejs/data/emp_engines/expresearch/category/*.json` and `research/*.json`, edited with `tools/researchedit` (see docs/QUESTS.md). A file holding only `{"retired": true}` is skipped (research removed from the pack).
- Stage locks: `pack/kubejs/data/emp_engines/chapters/stages/*.json` (generated by `tools/questgen/build_stages.py`).

### Research node format

```json
{
  "category": "emp_engines:automation",
  "title": "Andesite Age",
  "description": "What it unlocks",
  "icon": "create:andesite_alloy",
  "x": 0, "y": 0,
  "parents": ["emp_engines:some_other_node"],
  "xp": 160,
  "samples": [{ "item": "minecraft:andesite", "count": 32 }, { "item": "some:item", "keep": true }],
  "stages": ["emp_engines:andesite_age"]
}
```

Samples are used up, unless `"keep": true` (then the item only has to be in the researcher's inventory).

## Nether portal keys

Nether portals don't light at first:
1. Light a finished frame: the fire goes out with a hiss ("This doesn't seem to work..."), and the frame block under the fire turns into an **Obsidian Keyhole**. Right-click it for a hint.
2. Every ruined portal's chest holds a **Fragmented Obsidian Key** (a global loot modifier on `minecraft:chests/ruined_portal`, so Lootr chests too).
3. Research it (Metallurgy, after Alloy Age; the fragment is shown, not used up). That reveals the recipe: the fragment on a Tiered Foundry casting table with 360 mB of molten gold gives an **Obsidian Key** (the fragment is used up).
4. Use the key on the keyhole (it's used up): the portal lights normally from then on, even if it's broken and relit. Breaking the keyhole drops obsidian, and the next frame gets a new, locked keyhole.

There's no server-wide Nether lock: the keyholes are the gate. Portals in the Nether light freely. Both are switches in `[netherKeys]` of the server config. The casting recipe is hidden in EMI until researched; the casting table itself can't tell who's using it, so a player who already knows the recipe can cast a key without the research.

## Dungeons (placeholders)

Three dungeons in the Etheric Skies, each holding one fragment of the Ancient Eye Mold (Astral, Umbral, Gilded; putting the three together comes later): the Astral Sanctum, the Umbral Crypt and the Gilded Reliquary. ## Weathered Maps (treasure maps)

Each map leads to the nearest place of its kind within 64 chunks that no other map has claimed, so two maps never lead to the same one. Nothing in range: the map stays sealed ("The markings match nothing within reach of here").

## Does Enchanting Overhaul

Replaces random enchanting, Apothic Enchanting and Create: Enchantment Industry (`mods/researchenchanting`, mod id `researchenchanting`; spec: the Research Enchanting spec sheet). Stage 1: every vanilla enchantment plus Rending, Hookshot, reworked Smite and Boon of Arthropods.

- **The table**: the vanilla Enchanting Table, same screen layout and textures (UI packs restyle it). Whatever you put in stays in the table when you close it and floats above it, slowly bobbing and turning (a book or focus shows vanilla's open book instead); breaking the table drops it. Plain bookshelves count for nothing. When nothing can happen, the buttons say what to do (empty table; no focus books nearby for this item; research waiting on a resource). A plain book's tooltip says how to make a focus.
- **Echo (binding)**: a plain book in the table draws a random focus (foci already shelved around that table are skipped until all have been drawn). Click a button to begin: the three buttons light blue and chime in an order, then you click them back (your own presses light yellow). 3 rounds of 3, 4 and 5 steps (each round extends the last). A wrong press costs 1 lapis and replays the round. The steps play 12 ticks apart, +3 per 6 books in the chiseled shelves around (up to +12). Finished: the book becomes a Research Focus for 3 XP levels.
- **Research**: a focus in the table shows only its research tree, on a see-through panel over the three buttons (drag or scroll to pan; Esc closes the screen). When research is waiting on its resource, the title line says so. **A book holds one enchantment**: it researches up its line level by level, and starting a different line replaces what it holds (the node asks for a second click). Click the next level: it costs XP levels (1 per enchantment level) to start, then the table pulls that level's resource every 10 ticks from chests, barrels, vaults or any item handler up to 3 blocks out, from one below the table to one above, 10 x 4^(level-1) items (10, 40, 160, 640, 2560), at least 100 pulls. Each pull flies from the container into the book. Research Notes for that enchantment (same level or higher) pay a whole level. Another research can only start before anything is paid in. Progress lives on the book.
- **Enchanting**: any item in the table lists what the researched foci in the chiseled shelves around it hold (up to 3 blocks out, at the table's level or one up, with clear blocks between). Click a row to apply it: XP levels for the levels added, plus 1 lapis, paid at once. The menu closes and the table works on the item for 5 s (`enchantTicks`): it rises and spins up, the glyph ring tightens around it and glyphs pour in from the shelves, then a flash and the enchantment is on. The table can't be opened meanwhile; broken mid-way, it drops the item as it was and refunds the lapis and levels. Right-click lowers the level. Upgrades pay only the difference. Books are never used up.
- **Effects**: vanilla's enchanting glyphs wriggle on curving paths from every chiseled shelf holding a book into the book or item over the table (a trickle when you're near, more while research runs, a flood while enchanting), and while research runs they also trail from the chests it pulls from. Close to a table, a rune circle (`tools/art/research_enchanting.py`) turns on the ground around it with a ring of glyphs orbiting above; both brighten during research and enchanting.
- **Focus books** look like a brown book with a band in its gear's colour (swords red, bows orange, armour green, tools steel, tridents cyan, crossbows purple); each level researched adds gold to the band, and from level II a gold outline appears, faint at II and solid at V.
- **Foci (stage 1)**, six gear groups, data in `data/researchenchanting/research_enchanting/focus/*.json` in the jar (lines: enchantment, max level, resource, requirement, grid x/y; `stage` gates a focus behind a Chapters stage):

| Gear | Focus | Lines (resource) |
|---|---|---|
| Sword | Melee Offensive | Sharpness (iron), Sweeping Edge (string), Rending (flint), Density (cobbled deepslate), Breach (quartz) |
| Sword | Melee Utility | Knockback (slime), Hookshot (chain), Looting (emerald), Boon of Arthropods (cobblestone), Wind Burst (breeze rod) |
| Sword | Melee Elemental (after Exotic Age) | Fire Aspect (blaze powder), Smite (copper) |
| Bow | Archery | Power (arrows), Punch (slime), Flame (blaze powder), Infinity (arrows) |
| Crossbow | Trick Shots | Multishot (string), Quick Charge (redstone), Piercing (flint) |
| Trident | Tides | Impaling (prismarine shards), Loyalty (prismarine crystals), Riptide (kelp), Channeling (copper) |
| Armour | Armour Defensive | Protection (iron), Fire / Blast / Projectile Protection (blaze powder / gunpowder / arrows), Thorns (cactus) |
| Armour | Armour Mobility | Feather Falling (feathers), Soul Speed (soul sand), Swift Sneak (leather), Depth Strider (kelp), Frost Walker (snowballs), Respiration (prismarine shards), Aqua Affinity (prismarine crystals) |
| Tools | Tools Mining | Efficiency (redstone), Fortune (emerald), Silk Touch (string) |
| Tools | Tools Gathering | Luck of the Sea (emerald), Lure (cod) |

- **New and reworked enchantments**: Rending (each hit strips 2/3/4 armour for 6 s, stacking 3 hits); Hookshot (sneak + use: grapple to the block aimed at, 10/16/22 blocks, 4 s cooldown, no fall damage after); Smite (5/10/15% a hit calls lightning on the target, 8 s cooldown, never lights blocks; no longer conflicts with Sharpness); Boon of Arthropods (kills summon 1/2/3 silverfish that hunt monsters for 20 s, never burrow).
- **Removed**: Unbreaking and Mending (Silent Gear levels and repair kits cover durability): they fit nothing, do nothing, never appear in loot or trades, and are stripped from inventories.
- **Enchanted books** are gone: hidden in EMI, and those from loot (global loot modifier) and villager and wandering trader trades become Research Notes for their strongest enchantment.
- **Exotic Age** research now unlocks the Melee Elemental focus; its quests are Echo, First Research and Chosen Enchantment (hidden advancements `researchenchanting:bind_focus`, `research_node`, `enchant_item`).
- Textures from `tools/art/research_enchanting.py`. EMI: a binding page (book + lapis at the table: Research Focus), one page per researchable level (resource and amount, the focus book it makes), info pages for the table and Research Notes.

## Tiered Foundry

A foundry whose best yield comes from upgrading it, not from the first build (`mods/tieredfoundry`, mod id `tieredfoundry`; spec: the Tiered Foundry spec doc). Built so far: tiers 1 to 2 with casting, Silent Gear part casting and coating; it replaced Productive Metalworks and Silent Gear Metalworks. Not yet: the pressure lid (tier 2.5; tyrian steel alloys with fire bricks until then). Placeholder textures from Tinkers' Construct (MIT, `LICENSE-tinkers.txt` in the jar), not for release.

- **Structure**: the Foundry Controller sits in a wall, facing out. Behind it, an inside of air 1-7 by 1-7 and up to 8 tall. The floor under the inside is all heaters, one kind: all Solid Fuel Heaters or all Lava Heaters; around it, at the same level, a ring of bricks where Air Intakes can take the place of bricks along the sides. Every wall ring is bricks or Foundry Drains, and every corner, bottom ring included, is a wall of the foundry's bricks (vanilla Brick Wall, or Tiered Foundry's Fire Brick Wall in a fire brick foundry: 6 from 6 fire bricks, or 1 in a stonecutter). All the bricks must be one kind. The controller rechecks when a block in or around it is placed or broken (and every 5 s); its screen says what is wrong and where. Right-clicking any heater or drain of a formed foundry opens the controller. The controller screen is 176 wide like vanilla containers and shows only the fuel its heaters use (fuel slots or the lava gauge); melting progress fills each slot from the bottom. Heaters give light while burning (lava 14, solid 12), the controller (13) and drains (10) while there is metal, heat or something waiting to melt. Ponder scenes (hold W): building a foundry (controller, heaters, drain, fire bricks) and the air intake. The controller, drains and intakes are faces on the foundry's own bricks (`tools/art/tiered_foundry.py`, from vanilla bricks and lava). Items waiting to melt stand inside (one per inside block, shrinking as they melt) and the molten metal fills the inside from the floor, layers showing on the walls. While fans blow, smoke pours out of the open top: more with more fans, faster with more RPM, with sparks while it burns.
- **Tiers**: vanilla bricks are tier 1 (up to 1300°, ores ×1.2). A running Create encased fan blowing into an Air Intake (from below or outside) makes it tier 1.5 (ores ×1.4, alloying on, up to +300° at 256 RPM, less when slower). Fire Bricks make tier 2 (up to 1900°, Lava Heaters work, ores ×1.6). Controller, drains and intakes change their look to the tier; nothing needs replacing. Fire Bricks: Fire Clay (clay ball + sand + gravel, 2 from a Create mixer) goes on a casting table and is glazed with 250 mB of Molten Glass, which only a foundry makes (sand or glass melts at 1000°; 1000 mB a block, 250 a pane; 1000 mB in a basin casts glass). The recipe takes Tiered Foundry's own molten glass, not the c:molten_glass tag, so no other mod skips tier 1. Four Fire Bricks craft a block.
- **On ships (Sable)**: a foundry works on an assembled Create Aeronautics ship. Its interior and part links are stored relative to the block, so moving it into a ship's plot keeps them; smoke is projected to where the ship is (tieredfoundry/compat/sable/SableCompat, optional at runtime).
- **Heat**: Solid Fuel Heaters share one fuel store (4 slots in the controller screen; hoppers, funnels or a click with fuel on any heater fill it). Coal and coal blocks burn at 1000°, charcoal 900°, blaze rods 1100°, other furnace fuels 700°; fuel burns as fast as in a furnace for every 9 heaters (rounded up). Lava Heaters (fire brick foundries only) share one lava tank (4000 mB per heater, fill with Create pipes or buckets) and burn at 1600°, 10 mB/s for every 9 heaters (rounded up). The foundry warms 40°/s with the whole floor lit (slower when only part of it is). It loses 20°/s in brick, 12°/s in fire brick, divided by (1 + 0.4 × fans blowing in), shown when hovering the heat bar; heaters only burn while there is something to melt or alloy.
- **Melting**: put items in through the controller (its screen, or any hopper or funnel): one item per slot, one slot per inside block up to 63, 15 in view with a scrollbar. The screen is drawn from vanilla's GUI art, so UI packs restyle it. An item melts once the heat reaches its temperature, in 30 s at that temperature down to 5 s at 600° above it. Raw ores, ore blocks, raw blocks and Create's crushed ores melt to an ingot's worth (90 mB, blocks 810) times the tier's ore yield, so a crushing line stacks on top. Ingots, nuggets, plates and blocks melt 1:1. The foundry holds 1000 mB per inside block.
- **Metals**: iron 950°, gold 850°, copper 800°, zinc 420°, brass 900°, netherite and ancient debris 1500°, crimson iron 1100°, azure silver 1200°, blaze gold 1300°, azure electrum 1500°, crimson steel 1600°; tyrian steel 1800°, diamond 1450°; blaze (rods 400 mB, powder 100) 1000°, magma cream 900°, ender (pearls) 1000°, shulker (shells, 100 mB) 1200°. Tunable in `tools/foundry/gen.py`.
- **Alloying** (needs the fan): brass (copper 3 : zinc 1), netherite (debris 40 + gold 36 → 9), blaze gold (gold 90 + blaze 200 → 90), crimson steel (crimson iron 810 + blaze 200 + magma cream 100 → 270), azure electrum (azure silver 360 + gold 180 + ender 100 → 90), tyrian steel (crimson steel 90 + azure electrum 90 + shulker 100 + ancient debris 100 → 360, 1800°: for the pressure lid once it exists). Up to 10 batches every half second.
- **Pouring**: a Foundry Tap on a drain (or any tank) pours into the block below at 20 mB/tick when clicked or pulsed with redstone, until that block is full or the source is dry. Drains pour the bottom fluid in the controller screen first; click a fluid there to move it to the bottom. Buckets fill from drains.
- **Casting**: Casting Tables take a cast: Gold Casts (any ingot, nugget, plate, gem or Silent Gear part on the table under 180 mB of molten gold; reusable, melt back to 180 mB) or Sand Casts (put the item on the table and click it with sand: the item comes back, the sand cast breaks after one pour). Ingots 90 mB, nuggets 10, plates 90 (Create sheets), gems 90 (diamond), netherite scrap from 100 mB of ancient debris. Recipes can also use the item on the table itself as a one-off cast (the Obsidian Key fragment). Casting Basins make blocks (810 mB) and Fire Bricks. Metal cools in 20 ticks + 1 per 6 mB. Sneak-click a table or basin with a filled bucket or tank to accept only that fluid, sneak-click with an empty hand to clear. Hoppers and funnels take results out and can put casts in; Create spouts and pipes can fill them.
- **Silent Gear**: a part cast (made from any part of that kind: pickaxe head, sword blade, rod, tip, armour plates... 34 parts) filled with the part's number of ingots' worth (Silent Gear Metalworks' counts: pickaxe head 3, sword blade 2, rod 2, chestplate plates 8...) of a molten metal makes that part of that metal, if the metal is a material for it (the metal is found from what melts into 90 mB of it: an ingot or a gem). Any castable material, iron tier included; above iron it's the only way. Silent Gear gear on a table + 90 mB coats it with that metal and repairs it (the gear is the cast). Recipes: `tieredfoundry:part_casting`, data in the jar.
- **Settings**: `serverconfig/tieredfoundry-server.toml` (yields, heat limits, fuel and lava use, fan heat, melt times, slots, capacity, tap speed). EMI pages: melting, alloying, table and basin casting, making casts, and info on building the foundry.

## Sky villages

Create: Sky Village's two floating villages generate with their lowest block above Y 250 (start height 295-355, `pack/kubejs/data/create_sky_village/`), above the clouds; the overworld is 512 tall (top Y 448). They're protected: no placing or breaking blocks inside a sky village, and explosions leave them standing, but chests, doors and villagers work (`emp_engines`, `sky/SkyVillageProtection`, structure tag `emp_engines:sky_villages`; creative is exempt). Everywhere else there's no height limit: the old "nothing at or above Y 250" rule is gone, so tall mountains can be built on.

Road signs: Supplementaries' way signs generate near villages, pointing to the nearest villages with a rough distance (villages only, not other places).

## Diet (Diet - AppleSeed Edition)

Rewards only, set in `pack/config/appleseed-common.toml` (`Effects_Override`): each food group above 60% gives a small bonus and above 80% a bigger one, and they stack, so a balanced diet gets all five. Nothing happens when a group is low, and taking damage doesn't drain it.

| Group | 60-79% | 80-100% |
|---|---|---|
| Grains | +1 heart | +2 hearts |
| Fruits | +5% speed | +10% speed |
| Vegetables | +1 armour | +2 armour, +1 toughness |
| Protein | +0.5 attack damage | +1 attack damage |
| Sugars | +10% mining speed | +20% mining speed |

**Modded foods.** Diet works out each modded food's groups from its recipe, all the way down (Farmer's Delight cooking pot and cutting board included, through CloudWorks API's recipe templates), and caches the results in `config/apple_seed_foods/`. Diet itself only does this after a `/reload`, so Create: Empyrean Engines starts it on every server start (`compat/DietKickstart`); it only fills in foods without values, so it's quick after the first time. After adding or changing food mods, run `/diet cache regenerate` (op) to redo everything. Farmer's Delight feasts (roast chicken, stuffed pumpkin, honey glazed ham, shepherd's pie, gleaming salad) are served from the placed block, not crafted, so `pack/kubejs/data/emp_engines/recipe/diet/` gives Diet "simulated recipes" (one feast block = 4 servings). Placed pies and cheesecakes eaten bite by bite give no diet; cut them into slices on a cutting board. CloudWorks' debug dump (a file for every recipe on each server start, ~20 minutes of a background thread) is switched off by a mixin.

## Building a launcher (Create: Empyrean Engines)

1. Lay a flat rectangle (5x5 up to 33x33): quartz blocks in the middle, brass casing all round the edge (corners included).
2. Stand a Resonance Pylon on top of each corner. The lowest tier sets the run length.
3. Put the Navigation Console on top of the ring.
4. Hit a ring casing with a Create wrench. It opens into a Launcher Shaft Input on the axis of the face you hit (wrench again to turn it, sneak-wrench to get the casing back). Only works once the computer sees a finished platform.
5. Feed rotation into the input(s). Each input costs stress (inputStressImpact per RPM) and adds its speed / 8 to the charge every tick.
6. Right-click the console for its screen: run length and pylon tier, the ship's weight against the platform's limit (its area times `massPerTile`, 6.4 by default: 160 on 5x5, 518 on 9x9, ~7000 on 33x33; Sable's mass), charge, and the destination. A ship over the limit can't launch. When charged, give the console a redstone signal.
7. Optional: put **Navigational Data** in the console's slot before launch. The ship arrives right next to the structure it leads to, and the drive is used up. Drives turn up in ordinary structure chests (dungeons, mineshafts, temples, shipwrecks, outposts, mansions, strongholds, ancient cities, bastions, fortresses; 25%).

In game: hold W over the computer or a pylon for the Ponder scene. The scene's schematic and lang lines come from `tools/art/ponder_launcher.py`; the animated screen texture from `tools/art/terminal_textures.py` (both need Python with Pillow / nbtlib).

Block art is generated by scripts in `tools/art/` (Python with Pillow), so edit the script and rerun it rather than the JSON: `navigation_console.py` (the Navigation Console: a Create-style typewriter, as the Linked Typewriter from Create Aeronautics, with a brass monitor in place of the paper; keep `VectorPlanarComputerBlock`'s hitbox in step), `resonance_pylon.py` (the pylon base from Create's own brass casing, andesite casing and brass textures, and the pylon item models), `obsidian_keyhole.py` (plain vanilla obsidian with a small keyhole overlay on the four sides) and `research_table.py` (EXP Research's table as a vanilla-style job-site table: spruce body with dark oak trim, a blueprint, book and quill on top, books and scrolls on the front, a magnifying glass on the sides). The npm package block-model-renderer (with gl, headless-three, sharp and skia-canvas) can render any of them to a picture for checking.

Pylons: a Create-style base (brass-casing plinth, andesite collar, four brass posts, a brass socket) with a crystal shard floating above it and a broken gold ring orbiting the shard (amber tier 1, cyan tier 2, violet tier 3). The shard and ring are drawn in code (`client/PylonRenderer`). While the launcher charges the shards glow brighter, give off more light and the rings spin faster (blockstate `charge` 0-4, set by the computer). There are no other charging effects, just the quiet portal hum.

The launch:
1. Countdown (5 s): the ship lifts and levels.
2. Beams (4 s): light comes down from the sky onto each pylon's shard, then the beams' feet slide out (or in) to a circle round the ship, its radius half the ship's diagonal plus 2, so they never cut through it.
3. Spin (4 s): the beams circle faster and faster, smeared by ghost trails, and blend into one wall of light. The far side of the wall is bright and the near side faint, so the ship is a dark silhouette against it.
4. Launch (6.5 s): the ship goes up in a streak with fading afterimages and the wall collapses after it. It leaves at twice its average climbing speed, is still fast through the clouds (`launchApex`, default Y 256, above `cloudHeight` 224), then coasts for 3 s, slowing but never stopping, while the crew's view fades to white.

The beams are drawn after the clouds and weather; above the cloud base they skip depth testing so vanilla clouds can't hide them, and there's a soft flare on the cloud tops where they cross. Shaderpack clouds (Complementary's volumetric ones) are drawn later and can still cover the beams above the cloud layer.

## Etheric Skies terrain

The run dimension (`emp_engines:etheric_skies`) generates floating islands spread evenly: one island per cell of a bent, jittered grid (density function `emp_engines:island_cells`), so there are no clumps and no huge voids. Islands are up to about 290 blocks end to end (typically ~135), with roughly 20 to 245 blocks of sky between neighbours; tops between Y 105 and 196. Eleven biomes, each with its own tint, sky, fog, water, particles and rock, and its own island shape (blended by the same climate values that pick the biome, so shapes change smoothly at borders):

| Biome | Shape | Features |
|---|---|---|
| Amethyst Expanse (soft violet) | rounded domes | obsidian-amethyst shards bursting out of the ground, half-open geodes, glowing amethyst clusters, crystal points hanging underneath |
| Azure Wilds (soft blue) | rolling hills, deep undersides | dark-oak forest, moss and glow-berry vines under the islands, hanging roots, waterfalls into the void |
| Twilight Shards (soft indigo) | jagged peaks, ragged underside, a wide ring of debris | shards, open geodes, basalt columns, crystal points hanging underneath |
| Pale Remnant (snow and ice) | flat thin ice shelves | ice spikes, powder snow traps, icicles hanging underneath |
| Cherry Rift | rolling hills | a normal (vanilla) cherry grove, a rare giant cherry near the biome's middle, ponds with lily pads, waterfalls |
| Sky Meadow | gentle and flat | vanilla plains: oaks, flowers, farm animals |
| Sky Oakwood | rolling forest | vanilla oak and birch forest, podzol patches, wolves |
| Sky Desert | low rolling dunes | sand with red-sand patches over sandstone, cactus and dead bushes, desert wells, husks, rabbits and camels, sandstone points hanging underneath |
| Sky Jungle | tall lumpy hills, heavy undersides | vanilla jungle trees (big ones too), bamboo, melons, parrots, ocelots and pandas, moss underneath, waterfalls |
| Gilded Grove (gold) | raised mesas with cliffs | white-barked golden trees with beehives, sunflowers, sand and gravel paths, gold ore showing at the surface and small gold outcrops, rich gold ore, gold-cored points hanging underneath |
| Pale Forest (white) | low and even on top, long hanging points underneath | white trees and flowers, giant bones sticking out of the ground, cobwebs |

Each biome has its own subsoil instead of dirt (calcite, terracottas, deepslate, packed ice, sandstone...) and patches of other blocks on top; only Sky Meadow, Sky Oakwood and Sky Jungle keep dirt. The warm end of the map goes, driest to wettest: Sky Desert, Cherry Rift, Azure Wilds, Sky Jungle (about 6-7% of the land each). All the settings (island sizes and gaps, shape by biome, how often each feature appears, ores) are in `tools/worldgen/etheric.toml`, annotated. The client's Biome Blend is set to 11x11 by install-client so colours flow between biomes.

Structures, mismatched on purpose (a desert temple in a cherry forest): vanilla buildings set down on islands big enough for them, whatever the biome (structure type `emp_engines:on_island`, which checks the ground is there all round the spot out to a radius before letting the vanilla structure place itself).
- Common (one try every 12 chunks): desert temple, jungle temple, igloo, witch hut, ruined portal.
- Medium (every 22 chunks): pillager outpost, beached shipwreck, villages (plains, desert, savanna, snowy, taiga; they need a big island).
- Very rare (every 80 chunks): woodland mansion, only on the biggest islands.

Ores: coal, iron and copper (emerald in Twilight Shards and Pale Remnant) are the pack's own island versions: they sit in the island band (Y 60 to 190, most around 125) and replace the islands' coloured rock as well as stone (block tag `emp_engines:etheric_ore_replaceables`). Vanilla's spread over the whole world height and only replace stone, so on islands almost none landed.

Testing only: Silent Gear's endgame ores (crimson iron, azure silver, bort) the same way, only with Silent Gear installed (`neoforge/biome_modifier/test_silentgear_ores.json`).

All of it is vanilla world-generation data written by `tools/worldgen/build_etheric.py` from the settings in `tools/worldgen/etheric.toml`, which annotates every number: island size, spacing and shape, the large-island split, shards, biome size, the ore table (blocks, vein sizes, veins per chunk, height band), and each structure's island size and frequency. Edit it, run the script, rebuild the mod. Runs always generate fresh chunks, so a change shows on the next launch after a restart.

## Metals and the foundry

Written by `tools/recipes/build_recipe_overrides.py` into `pack/kubejs/data` (run it with the mods folder after updating Silent Gear or Crafts & Additions). Melting, alloying, casting and their temperatures are Tiered Foundry's own data, from `tools/foundry/gen.py`:

- **Alloys only come from the foundry.** Switched off: Create's brass mixing, Create Crafts & Additions' electrum mixing, vanilla's netherite ingot crafting, and Silent Gear's crafting-table alloys (blaze gold, crimson steel, azure electrum).
- **Silent Gear's Alloy Forge is gone** (the foundry alloys all its metals), and so is the Recrystallizer with Dimerald (a diamond-tier gem that could be made into parts by hand).
- **By hand only up to iron tier; no vanilla tools or armour.** Vanilla tools and armour (wooden to netherite, leather) have no recipes, the netherite smithing upgrades are off, and all of them are hidden in EMI. Iron tier and below, Silent Gear tools and armour are made straight from the material and sticks in the vanilla shapes (pickaxe, axe, shovel, hoe, sword, helmet, chestplate, leggings, boots: `emp_engines:sg_direct/*`), or the usual Silent Gear ways (blueprints and templates, rough tools). Every Silent Gear tool material above iron (harvest level over 2: diamond, netherite, crimson iron and steel, azure silver and electrum, tyrian steel, platinum, uranium, and the modded ones such as lumium, signalum, enderium, refined obsidian) gets the material category `emp_cast_only`, and every hand recipe that takes a head/plate, rod or tip material refuses it. Above iron the head (or plate, tip, rod) is cast in the foundry from a Tiered Foundry part cast, at the heat its metal needs, and the tool is the normal "head + rod" in the crafting grid. Recipe screens follow the same rules.
- **Armour looks.** Silent Gear armour of iron tier and below is drawn with the vanilla armour textures instead of Silent Gear's single full-cover plate texture (which looks like netherite): iron and gold exactly like vanilla iron and gold armour, cloth materials (leather, silk, membrane) like leather armour in their colour, turtle like the turtle shell, and the other iron-tier-or-lower materials (copper, bronze, steel, stone, wood...) like iron armour tinted to the material's colour. Armour above iron, or with a cast coating above iron, keeps Silent Gear's look. Item models follow too (inventory, in hand, item frames): a Silent Gear pickaxe, axe, shovel, hoe, sword or armour piece made of wood, stone, iron or gold (armour: leather, iron, gold; turtle helmet) uses the matching vanilla item's model; other materials keep Silent Gear's tinted model, so they can still be told apart. Client-side mixins on Silent Gear's armour item and the item renderer (Silent Apotheosis Raids, `client/VanillaArmorLook`).
- **Coating only by casting.** Put the Silent Gear tool, weapon or armour piece on a casting table and pour molten metal over it: it comes out coated with that metal and fully repaired, for one ingot's (or gem's) worth of fluid, the gear itself taken as the cast (Tiered Foundry, `compat/silentgear/SgCasting`). Silent Gear's own coating recipes (the coating part, its blueprint and template, the coating smithing template and the coating smithing recipes) are gone and their items hidden. EMI has an info page for it on the casting table (it isn't a fixed recipe: it's worked out from the gear you put down).
- **Vanilla-looking icons:** a Silent Gear sword, tool or armour piece of a vanilla material draws with the vanilla item's model and colours (Silent Gear's material tint is skipped for it; leather armour gets vanilla's undyed brown).
- **Vanilla gear becomes Silent Gear.** Vanilla tools, weapons and armour (wooden, stone, iron, golden, diamond and netherite tools and armour, and leather armour; chainmail has no Silent Gear counterpart and stays vanilla) turn into their Silent Gear counterparts as soon as they're in a player's inventory or armour slots (picked up, looted, traded, taken from a chest; not in creative), keeping enchantments, name and lore, with wear carried over as the same fraction of durability. The conversions are Silent Gear's own, moved to `pack/kubejs/data/silentraids/gear_conversion/` by the script so there's no crafting-grid conversion and nothing extra in recipe screens (Silent Apotheosis Raids, `gear/VanillaToSilent`).
- **Heat:** diamond-tier metals (diamond, netherite and ancient debris, azure electrum, crimson steel, tyrian steel) melt at 1450-1800°, so they need a fire brick foundry with lava heaters; crimson iron (1100°), azure silver (1200°) and blaze gold (1300°) need a fan in a brick one.
- **Recipe screens:** ("removed" in this pack always means no recipe and hidden in EMI.) EMI shows the foundry (melting, alloying, table and basin casting, Silent Gear part casting per metal) through Tiered Foundry's own EMI plugin, and Silent Gear's gear crafting and the Stone Anvil recipes through TooManyRecipeViewers, which runs their JEI-only plugins inside EMI without JEI. Chapters' own JEI plugin is switched off in `pack/config/tmrv_plugins.json5` (EXP Research's EMI plugin hides locked items instead). Silent Gear in EMI is per material (Silent Apotheosis Raids, `compat/emi/GearRecipesEmi`): gear and parts are told apart by their main material, and every Silent Gear crafting recipe is shown again for each material it accepts, with the real result. So a diamond sword shows diamond blade + rod, the diamond blade shows its casting (on the Table Casting pages, one per part and metal), and an iron sword also shows the direct iron recipe and the blueprint recipes. Recipes it can't use (iron-tier material recipes for diamond gear) no longer show on it.
- **Upgrading gear:** gear + a part in the crafting grid swaps that part (Silent Gear's swap recipe): a new rod (the handle), tip, grip (the hilt wrap: leather, wool, cloth), binding, cord, fletching, lining or setting. EMI shows a page per gear item and part type. Rods and tips of any castable metal are cast at the foundry (rod and tip casts), then swapped on.
- **Tier upgrades:** the same swap works for the head or plates: iron helmet + cast diamond helmet plates = diamond helmet, keeping enchantments, name, levels and gem sockets. EMI has a page per gear item and material above iron (the diamond helmet shows "iron helmet + diamond plates"). Coating by casting is in EMI too (Silent Gear Casting and Coating: gear + one ingot's worth of molten metal = coated gear, per gear item and metal); a coating changes the look and adds its coating stats, but the plates underneath stay what they were.
- **Hidden in EMI:** the Alloy Forge, Recrystallizer, Silent Gear's custom ingot/gem (their recipes are gone too), along with every recipe that uses them. `pack/kubejs/server_scripts/foundry_overrides.js` (also written by the script) removes the same recipes by id as a second line of defence and does the EMI hiding.

## One item per metal (Almost Unified)

Create and Crafts & Additions each add their own copper/iron/gold sheets, wires, rods and electrum. Almost Unified picks one per tag and rewrites every recipe to output it; the others are hidden in EMI (old ones in chests still work, since recipes take the tag). Priority: Minecraft, then Create, then Crafts & Additions, so you get Create's sheets and nuggets and Crafts & Additions' wires, rods and electrum. Silent Gear's items are left alone.

Config: `pack/config/almostunified/placeholders.json` (materials: copper, iron, gold, electrum) and `pack/config/almostunified/unification/materials.json` (tags: ingots, nuggets, plates, wires, rods, storage blocks). Add a material there to unify more.

## Silent Apotheosis Raids

**Raid Elites**: Apotheosis 8.9 only has skeleton Elites (Frontier and up), so the pack adds its own named ones for raid mobs at every tier (pack/kubejs/data/silentraids/apothic_elites/raids, from tools/raids/elites.py; success chance 0, so raids only). A raider whose type still has no Elite gets our own stand-in boost. Every raid Elite is then renamed with Apotheosis's random name generator (the Invaders' names), keeping the Elite's colour; "Elite <mob>" only if that generator is missing.

**Night Raids** (the code still calls them blood moons). Every 5th night in the Overworld (`everyNights`). At dusk the evening before everyone gets a chat warning. On the night a red "A Night Raid is coming" bar fills from 11000 to nightfall (13000), and beds don't work from 12000. While the moon is up every player sees a red moon and a blood-tinted night sky, clouds and fog (client side, fades in and out; with a shader pack the moon and sky tint still show but the shader may soften them). From nightfall (13000) to dawn every player who is online, in the Overworld and on the ground gets a siege. Players on a Sable ship, flying or gliding get no waves, and a siege waits while all its players are up there. Players within 48 blocks of each other share one siege (bigger waves). Raiders spawn 30-40 blocks away, always go for the nearest player of their siege, and when they can't reach them they break in: 30 s per block, with cracks showing, and the players hear "You hear them breaking into your walls...". They only break plain `minecraft:` blocks without a block entity: never chests, furnaces, beds or any other container, never a block from Create, Create: New Age, the foundry or another mod, and never obsidian or anything in `#silentraids:siege_proof`. Raider creepers' explosions follow the same rule. Raider equipment never drops. Each siege has a raid-style red bar instead of on-screen text: "Night Raid - Wave N of M approaching" fills for 10 s before each wave (the raid horn sounds as it comes in), then "Night Raid - Wave N of M - Raiders: X" drains as that wave's raiders die; the next wave starts its countdown once 75% of the current one is dead (or after `waveTimeout`), and the bar turns gold with "Survived!" when the last raider of the final wave dies. At dawn the survivors vanish. Beating every wave gives a Sigil of Socketing.

**Threat** = world tier (0-4) + progress through that tier's hours (0-0.99), so it rises within a tier too. Waves: 1-2 in Haven, 2-3 Frontier, 3-4 Ascent, 4 Summit, 5 Pinnacle. Wave size 3 + 2 x threat + wave number (x1.4 per extra player, capped at `maxAlive` alive). Haven is zombies, skeletons and spiders; creepers from 0.5, husks, strays and zombie villagers from Frontier, witches from 1.5, vindicators and pillagers from Ascent, wither skeletons from Summit, evokers from 3.5, a ravager in Pinnacle's last wave. Zombies and skeletons wear armour, more pieces and better metal as threat rises (leather to netherite), enchanted from Summit; every raider gets +15% health and +10% damage per point of threat. Every blood moon brings at least one Elite: if none has come by the final wave, its first raider is one (in Haven too). From Frontier more raiders can be Elites (Apotheosis Elites where one fits the mob, otherwise a gold-named Elite with 2.5x health); from Ascent the last wave brings an Apotheosis Invader (two at Pinnacle).

**World tiers** are automatic and per player (manual changes are off in `pack/config/apotheosis/apotheosis.cfg`): Frontier at 5 h of play, Ascent at 15 h and having been to the Nether, Summit at 30 h and having killed the Wither (or built a beacon), Pinnacle at 60 h and having been to the End. Tiers never go down; a promotion shows a title.

**Gems only.** No Apotheosis affix gear: affix conversion and affix chest loot are 0 (`pack/kubejs/data/apotheosis/loot_modifiers`), the random-affix entries in Apotheosis' own chest tables are gems instead, affix items are stripped from every mob drop and from Wandering Trader offers. The Reforging, Simple Reforging and Augmenting Tables are gone (recipes off, hidden in EMI); the Salvaging Table stays because it grinds gems into dust for gem cutting. Raid Elites drop a gem (10% a sigil), Invaders 2 + threat/2 gems (50% a sigil). Apotheosis' Elites and Invaders out in the world drop gems but no sigils.

**Sigils of Socketing** can't be crafted: they come from night raids (beating all waves, Elites, Invaders) and quests (the Night Raids & Gems chapter, and entering the Nether). On the smithing table (gear in the middle, sigil on the right) a sigil adds one socket, once per item (`silentraids:sigil_socket` replaces Apotheosis' recipe).

**Silent Gear levels** (tools, weapons, armour; not curios). XP: 1 per block mined with the right tool, 4 per ore; damage dealt to monsters (plus a kill bonus); armour: damage its wearer takes from mobs. XP per level = 100 x (level + 1)^1.5. Every level: +5% to durability, mining speed, attack damage and speed, magic/ranged damage, draw and projectile speed, armour, toughness, knockback resistance and magic armour (applied after Silent Gear recalculates, so it survives repairs, part swaps and upgrades). Levels 3, 6 and 9 add a gem socket. Level cap by tier: 3 / 5 / 7 / 9 / 10; XP keeps counting at the cap. The Silent Gear Salvager refuses gear with levels or socketed gems. The tooltip shows level and XP.

Config: `serverconfig/silentraids-server.toml` (interval, beds, spawn radius, sharing radius, max alive, wave timeout, sigil chance, break time, tier hours, level caps, socket levels, XP rates, stat bonus).

## Sky Raiders (prototype)

Boarding raids against player ships. They start on their own (`raid/NaturalRaids`):
- Only against players on a ship at or above Y 230 in the Overworld (`raidMinY`, above the clouds at 224), so low transport and test flights never get raided, or at any height in the Etheric Skies (`raidMinYEtheric`). Not in creative or spectator.
- Each player has a clock of time spent up there since their last raid, kept through logouts. Every 10 s they roll with a chance that rises with the clock: rare just after a raid, about even odds by an hour, close to certain by two. On average one raid per player-hour (`raidAverageMinutes`, 60). A raid hits the ship that player is on and resets the clock of everyone aboard, so a crew of 2 is raided about every 42 minutes, 3 about every 35.
- Bad Omen (drink an Ominous Bottle) on a ship high enough brings a raid at once and uses up the effect.
- At most `raidMaxActive` (2) raids at once; a ship where a raid couldn't start (no open deck, no room to bring the raider in) is tried again after a minute; only configured, enabled raider ships come (none set up = no raids).

`/skyraid start` still starts one by hand. Design and feasibility: https://claude.ai/artifact/HrC4hwoWzD4H23NTJRCbto

Raider ships are real airships from the pack's structure mods (config raidShips lists them, e.g. cr_aero_structures:pillager_airballoon). Before a ship can raid it's set up once in the gallery with the marker kit. Operators only:

    /skyraid gallery                      every ship in a row in the sky near you, in a quartz outline with a name tag
    /skyraid gallery <ship>               just that one          /skyraid gallery tp <ship>   go to it
    /skyraid gallery clear                take the gallery down
    /skyraid config <ship>                start: places it if needed, gives the marker kit, puts saved markers back
    /skyraid config <ship> save           save, take the marker blocks away, show the stats
    /skyraid config <ship> cancel|reset   stop without saving | forget its setup
    /skyraid config <ship> speed <b/s>    top speed (default by size: 22 under 600 blocks, 18 under 1500, else 15)
    /skyraid config <ship> turn <deg/s>   turn rate (default 25)
    /skyraid ships                        every ship and whether it's ready
    /skyraid ships info <ship>            its stats
    /skyraid ships enable|disable <ship>  in or out of the raid pool
    /skyraid start [ship]                 raid the ship you're on (a random ready ship, or that one)
    /skyraid status | overwhelm | stop    the raid on your ship

The marker kit is tagged concrete (ordinary concrete never counts), placed inside the ship's outline:
- Helm (red): exactly one. The way you face when you place it is the ship's front. The captain stands here.
- Port Hook (black) and Starboard Hook (green): rope connectors on the ship's left and right. A raid uses one side's hooks: that side faces you (either side at random if both have hooks).
- Raider Post (light gray): one raider each. About a quarter (at least one) are archers who stay aboard and shoot; the rest board.
- Witch Post (purple, optional), Loot Hold (brown, optional: a barrel goes here; else the first barrel or chest).

Saving also keeps the ship as it stands in the gallery: blocks you add or take away while configuring go with it to raids (`<name>.nbt` next to the json; reset deletes both). Saved setups are `config/emp_engines/raider_ships/<name>.json` in the game folder. `tools/pull-raider-ships.ps1` copies them (json and nbt) from the CurseForge profile into `pack/config` (install-client does it first, so a newer one in the profile is never overwritten); commit them to ship them. Only configured, enabled ships raid.

A raid:
0. Warning, like a village raid: the raid horn sounds from the direction the raiders will come, and a "Sky Raid" bar at the top fills over 10 s before the ship comes in. After that the bar shows the crew's remaining health ("Raiders Remaining: N") and falls as you kill them; it ends as Victory, Defeat (they got loot away) or Raiders Escaped.
1. The raider ship is brought in out of view (well off to the side and behind, keeping pace during the warning), already facing your way, with its crew on their posts: archers (crossbow pillagers, shooting from 40 blocks), boarders (vindicators and pillager looters, alternating), the captain (a pillager with the ominous banner) at the helm and any witches.
2. It flies by "magic" physics: velocity set directly, top speed in about 4 s, turns no faster than its turn rate, kept level, no sag, climbs over anything ahead (or below). Its engines, throttles and wheel only put on a show. It comes up on whichever side puts its hooks towards you.
3. Once alongside it locks on: where it sits relative to your ship is captured, and every tick it's put exactly there and given your ship's velocity and spin. You can't shake it off. Then the boarding ropes go out, each from a hook on the side facing you to the open deck spot nearest it. A boarding rope is drawn with Create Aeronautics' own rope model but has no physics (a Simulated rope pulled both ships hard at speed), hanging in a shallow curve (raidRopeSag, 6% of its length). `/skyraid status` says whether the hooks face you (if not, the helm marker's facing is wrong).
   Players can walk the ropes too, to board the raider: step or jump onto one and you're held on top and carried along with it as the ships fly; sneak to drop off. The server doesn't count it as flying (mixin on its anti-fly check).
4. Boarders walk from their posts to the hooks and cross one by one; the captain always boards, last. Boarders have 70% knockback resistance, so they're hard to knock off. Only boarders on deck count towards overwhelming your crew (raidOverwhelmMargin, or nobody aboard for 20 s); then looters take up to raidStacks stacks each back into the hold. Kill them first and they drop it.
5. Cutting the ropes: the anchors at either end are the only weak point. The rope itself can't be cut (it isn't a block or a Simulated rope), shears are refused and explosions spare the connectors. Breaking one takes 3 s whatever the tool (raidAnchorSeconds), 10 s with Mining Fatigue (raidAnchorFatigueSeconds); taking damage doesn't interrupt it; creative is instant.
6. The witch stays aboard and, once a rope is tied, every 7 s throws a splash potion of Mining Fatigue I (20 s) at whoever is breaking an anchor (or anyone within 4 blocks of one), up to 16 blocks away; it splashes whoever is beside them too. Milk clears it. She still drinks her own healing potions.
7. When every rope is cut the lock lets go and it flees (at 75% speed if it got loot, so it can be chased), deleted after 20 s (90 s with loot).
8. When all the boarders are dead: if an archer is left, "The raiders are getting away!" and it walks to the helm; 10 s later it casts off (ropes cut) and flees. Kill it first (any other archer takes over) or cut the ropes. With no archers left the ship is a derelict: it stays alongside while a rope is up so you can loot it, then drifts once the ropes are gone and is removed 3 minutes later.

Loot aboard is Lootr's (everyone gets their own): loot chests and barrels in a ship's design become Lootr ones, and the Loot Hold marker is a Lootr barrel with pillager outpost loot. Stolen cargo goes in a plain barrel placed on top of the hold.

Raiders that fall or are knocked more than raidLostDistance (32) blocks clear of both ships are dropped from the raid (removed; they count as dead on the bar).

Config: `[raids]` in emp_engines-server.toml. Every setting is annotated in `pack/defaultconfigs/emp_engines-server.toml` (new worlds get a copy; an existing world uses `<world>/serverconfig/emp_engines-server.toml`).

## Performance

- **Distant Horizons** is capped by install-client: at most a quarter of the CPU threads, thread priority 3 (below Minecraft's), far generation within 2048 blocks (it only lowers values). With 20 threads at normal priority its far-terrain generation starved Minecraft's own chunk generation on sky runs and the server stalled for seconds at a time.
- **Create Aeronautics: Discovery flyovers** spawn per 512-block area with a 20-minute cooldown (`pack/config/aeronauticsdiscovery-common.toml`; the defaults are 128 blocks and 10 minutes). Spawning one loads and generates its chunks on the server thread, which froze the server for up to 40 s at a time while flying fast through new terrain.

## UI theme

`/uitheme grey|light|dark` (client command) picks the menu and HUD look: grey is vanilla, light is Recolourful Containers, dark is Recolourful Containers DARK (the default). It only switches those resource packs, so Options > Resource Packs does the same. install-client switches each resource pack on only the first time it appears (remembered in `emp_resourcepacks_seen.txt` in the profile), and never switches on the light one, so a chosen theme sticks across updates.

## Known gaps in this first version

- Launch visuals: soft additive beams and a wall of light drawn by LaunchRenderer after the clouds, a streak with afterimages, a ripple on the cloud tops, camera shake and a white-out. Post-processing effects (distortion, bloom) are left out because they break under Iris shaderpacks.
- Every launch uses its own far-away region of one shared Etheric Skies dimension, not a separate dimension.
- The team treasury is not in yet: claims are paid by the clicking player, rent by the team owner.
- Players who log out in the Etheric Skies and come back after it collapsed are not yet killed.
- Ship lift and launch depend on Sable's physics and need tuning in game.
- FTB Chunks' own maximum-claims setting still applies; raise it in FTB Chunks' server config.
